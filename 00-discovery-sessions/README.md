# 00 Discovery Sessions

Conversations preserved exactly as they occurred.

## Why this exists

Ideas do not arrive finished. They arrive sideways, in the middle of some other task, disguised as a digression. The Architecture for Creation emerged during an attempt to rewrite a short biography for a think tank board page, which is not a thing anyone would have predicted or planned.

That means the process of discovery is itself evidence. How an idea actually formed, what it was mistaken for first, which objection forced the correction, which offhand sentence turned out to carry the whole thing: none of that survives a summary. A summary keeps the conclusions and discards the reasoning that earned them, which is precisely backwards for a repository whose purpose is to be a source rather than a product.

These files are primary historical sources. They are the only things here that cannot be reconstructed later. Everything else in this repository can be rewritten from them; they cannot be rewritten from anything.

## What belongs here

Complete conversation transcripts, one markdown file per session, each with metadata at the top:

- title
- date
- origin, meaning what the conversation was actually trying to do when it started
- participants
- surprising observations
- major breakthroughs

The metadata block is the concession to future readability. It exists so that a reader can locate a session without reading it, and it is the only editorial layer permitted. Where the transcript's own timestamps disagree with a remembered date, the timestamps win.

## What does not belong here

**Do not rewrite. Do not summarize. Do not clean up.** No fixing the typos, no tightening the rambling passage, no cutting the twenty minutes spent on something that went nowhere.

The mistakes, false starts, corrections, emotional reactions, and side quests are the record. A false start shows where the obvious answer was and why it failed, which is information the polished version no longer contains. A correction shows what force was strong enough to change a mind. A side quest is often where the next distinction is hiding, unrecognized.

Also not here: analysis of a conversation, notes about what it meant, or extracted conclusions. Those are real work, but they belong in `06-distinctions/`, `07-principles/`, `08-questions/`, or `10-narrative/`. Keep the source layer pure so that later thinking can always be checked against it.

## How it relates

This folder feeds everything. Distinctions are named here first, usually clumsily. Principles are lived here before they are stated. The narrative in `10-narrative/` traces its chronology through these sessions, and the Kernel in `09-kernel/` should ultimately be traceable, claim by claim, back to a passage in one of them.

When a later document and a session transcript disagree about what was said, the transcript is right.
