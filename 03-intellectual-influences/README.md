# 03 Intellectual Influences

What was taken, from whom, and what it made visible.

## Why this exists

No architecture is built from nothing. This one is assembled from tools borrowed across physics, economics, systems theory, operations, law, and the transformational tradition, and the borrowing is not incidental. A distinction picked up from Ohno on a factory floor turns out to apply to a legal practice; a habit of mind learned from Feynman turns out to govern how a business problem gets approached. The cross-domain transfer is a large part of what the Architecture actually is.

Recording the influences does three things. It keeps the intellectual debts honest, which matters for a body of work that will eventually be published. It preserves the specific idea that was taken, which is usually much narrower and sharper than the thinker's reputation. And it makes the lineage of each distinction traceable, so that a reader can tell where a claim came from and judge it accordingly.

## What belongs here

Notes, excerpts, and reflections on thinkers whose ideas shaped the Architecture. Among them:

Richard Feynman, Werner Erhard, Brian Arthur, Jay Forrester, Herbert Simon, John Holland, Elinor Ostrom, Taiichi Ohno, Eliyahu Goldratt, Michael Porter, and others as they arise.

For each, capture the specific ideas that did work here: the distinction that changed what could be seen, the sentence that reframed a problem, the method that got adopted. Quote precisely and cite where it came from. Note what it made visible that was invisible before, and note honestly where the borrowing departs from the original, since a productive misreading is still worth recording as a misreading.

The list above is a starting set, not a boundary. An influence that arrives from an unexpected quarter belongs here on the same terms.

## What does not belong here

**Do not summarize entire books.** A book summary is a substitute for reading and contributes nothing this repository needs. The unit here is the idea that influenced the Architecture, not the work it came from.

Do not collect thinkers for authority. A name that lends prestige but did no actual work in the thinking is decoration, and decoration in a source folder is a liability. If nothing here traces back to a given thinker, that thinker is not yet an influence.

Once an idea has been absorbed, adapted, and made this project's own, it graduates. The mature version lives in `06-distinctions/` or `07-principles/`, and this folder keeps the record of where it came from.

## How it relates

Upstream of `06-distinctions/` and `07-principles/`, which is where borrowed ideas end up once they have been reworked. A distinction in `06` should be able to name its ancestry here, and a distinction with no ancestry is either original or unexamined, which is worth knowing either way.

Also a check on originality. Reading this folder should make it obvious which parts of the Architecture are inherited and which are genuinely new, and that boundary is one of the more valuable things the repository can know about itself.
