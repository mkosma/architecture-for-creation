Objective:  To improve the efficiency and effectiveness of the practice of law and drive strong and sustainable competitive advantage as an operations leader in a corporate legal department.

I am strongly motivated by a passion for transformation and for translating data into actionable knowledge; these hallmarks help define and illuminate each step in my career.

My pre-law school career focused on using cutting-edge technology to transform both scientific and business processes.  At Lockheed, I applied my physics and computer simulation skills to develop supercomputer software for stealth aircraft design.  Within a short period of time, I became one of Lockheed’s two experts on massively parallel algorithms, exploiting Thinking Machines’ Connection Machine to run circles around Lockheed’s top-of-the-line Cray Y-MP.    As the Cold War ended, I joined a startup, where I created a system to capture and analyze the skills of individuals and groups of knowledge workers.  Our team applied those tools to help PepsiCo identify high-potential candidates for promotion to SVP.  This opened my eyes to the opportunity to transform businesses using data to drive decisions and technology to enhance operations.  I followed up on that interest with further business technology projects, such as helping transform Butler Manufacturing’s sales operations with a system allowing field representatives to sit side-by-side with customers, design and customize buildings, and print initial engineering plans on the spot.

My growing interest in complex business problems led me to law school.  At the University of Chicago, I developed a love of economics, and won the Olin Prize for the outstanding graduate in law and economics.  Bringing together my interests in transformation and analytics, I developed an investment model of the selection of Supreme Court nominees, and analyzed patterns in millions of citations in Supreme Court and Court of Appeals decisions.  That study, published in a leading economics journal, revealed that older nominees have had more influence on the development of precedent, despite their shorter average tenure on the bench.

I joined the competition practice at Gibson Dunn & Crutcher to pursue my interest in regulation of technology products and markets.  Exploiting my engineering background, I worked for a variety of clients on technology-related counseling and litigation matters.  For example:

Advised Cadence Design Systems on numerous technology, IP, and competition issues; managed compliance with an FTC consent decree; counseled client on opportunities and risks in industry standard setting; drafted documents and filings relating to multiple international mergers; advised on its software product portfolio strategy in light of likely regulatory response; developed and presented to FTC arguments on likely effects of transactions

Core member of the Intel antitrust team on numerous matters including the major FTC investigation, where I was primarily responsible for fact issues relating to microprocessor technology and competition between Pentium and Digital’s Alpha

Lead associate for Ralston Purina in the acquisition by Nestle, responsible for supervising legal and factual research, extensive drafting and negotiation of agreements, presentations to U.S. and international regulators, econometric analysis of prices and trends in numerous consumer product markets, and analysis of the impact of category managers on retail sales channels

Advised BMC Software on litigation and adjustments to business practices including trade secrets protection, multi-product bundling, structured discount contracts, and integration among software products

Second-chaired and won a case overturning FCC regulations permitting warrantless surveillance of packet-based communications and imposing costly requirements on telecom providers, on behalf of the Center for Democracy and Technology and the wireless industry association (CTIA)

To increase my opportunity to work on forward-looking projects of strategic import to clients, I joined the competition practice at Jones Day, one of the top three M&A practices in the world.  I continued to work on a wide variety of transactions and business practices.  For example:

For a Fortune 50 client, advised chief procurement officer on negotiations with suppliers, and worked with in-house contracting team to develop terms for transactions with local government customers

Advised the leading floral delivery network on e-commerce issues, including terms of service, privacy protections, and compliance with multi-state and federal regulations applying to financial and personally identifying information; also provided guidance on sharing of customer data and other contract issues with local floral delivery partners

Worked extensively with P&G on numerous transactions, managing merger filings in the U.S. and multiple international jurisdictions, and successfully achieving global approval for its acquisitions of Gillette and Wella; developed deep experience in market research across numerous consumer products areas and strong working relationships with data providers IRI and Nielsen

Advised multiple clients on internal investigations within procurement organizations, including interviewing executives, leading forensic teams, and advising GCs on risk assessment and mitigation

Advised numerous clients on standard-setting processes, by-laws, and agreements, including corporations, industry associations, and NGOs, across areas such as XML data interchange standards, internet domain naming, and “green” appliances

Represented a brokerage on false advertising claims arising from a national TV commercial, as counsel of record in the U.S. Supreme Court and the 11th Circuit

Having experienced first-hand the difficulty in transforming substantive law, and the lack of interest by law firms in reengineering their own operations, I decided to leave the practice of law to become a McKinsey consultant.

In my time with McKinsey, I found a much stronger fulfillment of my desire to participate in cutting costs and driving growth through innovation.  The highlight was working side-by-side with clients as a member of broader teams, working business and legal executives on issues involving merger synergies analysis, post-merger integration, law department organization structure, and information processing workflows.

In 2008, I was recruited by former ABA Chairman and Montsanto General Counsel Bill Ide to join a startup, MetaJure, to build a business based on a McKinsey model of providing high-end advice to corporations on law department operations.  Together with a team that included former PepsiCo General Counsel Dave Andrews and Microsoft Chief Operations Counsel Kevin Harrang, I served as a key outside advisor to Intellectual Ventures on IP risk management, litigation readiness, and legal and information management operations.  In the summer of 2009, the startup recruited a new CEO, shifted its focus, and abandoned its vision of helping GCs reengineer legal operations.

Strategically, this made sense:  GCs (especially in this economy) tend to have pressing problems that they need solved, and don’t have the luxury of spending money on consulting studies that deliver mere recommendations.  Accordingly, in Fall of 2009 I joined CPA Global as a Senior Managing Consultant, and have since been promoted to Vice President of Legal Services Outsourcing.

In my current role, I am one of the leaders in bringing to market a new model of legal services business.  My principal responsibility is to help corporate legal executives understand and adopt new ways of approaching the acquisition and provision of legal services.  I function at the intersection of strategy, business development, and operations, helping corporations realize the promise of effective legal operations management by bringing together process engineering, legal expertise, and technology.  As a result of my work, clients are slashing their spend on outside counsel and freeing up in-house attorneys for higher value tasks – taking work back from their law firms, and spending more time as strategic partners with the business.

Greatest strengths:  ability to build effective cross-disciplinary teams, to speak the language of engineers and technologists and translate into the realm of business and law, to identify opportunities for synergies across products, systems, organizations, and people, and to help navigate the implementation of strategy in real-world operations

Greatest weaknesses: often grasp new concepts and ideas and see potential opportunities more quickly than colleagues, so need to be conscious to slow down and communicate effectively to ensure my team is moving ahead together; sometimes too direct and passionate about quality and strong results, needing to adjust my enthusiasm and tone to ensure that I bring people together around an important business need instead of coming across as critical.
